# Arrival

Experimental Logs for Grav Gate Project
*N. Oleander*

## Day 1:

Gate stabilization was challenging due to unforeseen asymmetries. The readings suggest better performance and stability
out of the chiral approach, which is silly because of how little sophistication it entails. If it's *possible* to build
a chiral loop teleporter, then you can pretty much build it out of wet garbage. Still, we've proven our technology: it
is possible to create a gate to... somewhere.

Where it goes is a big question. Our Geneva lab was not able to lock on to our anchor signal, which also tracks with the
strong asymmetries we've observed. We need a lot more instrumentation that I simply do not have funding for, and without
that we're kind of dead reckoning off the back of a napkin. And if I didn't screw up the numbers somewhere, that napkin
says this gate travels in a direction that is not space-like, it's not time-like, it's an "extra" dimension that we
thought was just a compact space.

Distance isn't really supposed to matter in this compact space, but the far end of the gate was cast *really* far. So
we've got to thinking, what if this compact space is connected to other spaces? We think this is where the nonlinearity
came from: if we're a disproportionately small number of connections into this space it might have "wicked" our little
gate experiment up towards the multiply-connected lamp, as it were.


## Day 2:

We spent a tense first 28 hours tweaking gravitic flux numbers and logging our findings, sleeping under our desks as
much as we could and hoping the gate remained stable. It did. And as the data continues to accumulate, our certainty
grows that this is a new universe.

As such we have a solemn responsibility to explore and understand this new space. Once we fine-tuned the gate stability,
I ducked out and acquired some cheap drones we can send through the gate. This could easily be a bust, the gate may
have anchored to a random point in outer space (in fact it is overwhelmingly likely that it is a random spot in space!).
All the more reason to do automated reconnaissance before we send a person out there.

I've been awake for way too long. I've just stepped away a moment with I. Setosa at the controls. She is performing
admirably against the lag and the static. I worry that there is not much more we can learn from this approach.

We've been unable to retrieve any drones. Not a single one. We can send more material over, but we can't bring anything
back. So we've cobbled together environmental test kits - because, against all odds, we have not only discovered a new
universe, but a **planet**!

Our initial experiments were quickly stymied by the need for color recognition - something about the atmosphere or the
sun (or suns?!?) makes the ambient light spectrum weird. So we had to abandon our initial chemical tests because we
couldn't match test strips against the color chart. That wasted *hours* because these drones were made to take cool
aerial videos and not to perform fine motor tasks!

Speaking of aerial footage, or initial plan, on finding a planet, was to take some footage and get a sense of the area.
But something weird is happening with the space. Anything right next to the gate is "connected", but venturing further,
we don't have signal. Even right next to the gate as we're working, things are choppy and hard to make out.

## Day 5:

With a little ingenuity we were able to repeat our chemical tests, and get results. The soil is... soil? It has lots of
silicon, and a surprising amount of complex carbon. There is *oxygen* in the atmosphere, at just about the right partial
pressure to be breathable. We still have to rule out outright toxins, but I think we've hit the jackpot of a planet,
in the goldilocks zone of its sun, with a breathable atmosphere, and maybe even life with all this free complex carbon?

Preposterous. It's too good to be true.

## Day 6:

The slagging Deltways lab made a chiral loop teleporter work all the way from New Hampshire to Neo London. Our funding's
been cut. I've bargained with the university to buy enough time to finish reading instruments and shut down the gate
safely. The team is inconsolable. We found a new **universe** and nobody cares, because you can't use it to speed up
delivery for packages of luxury electronics or fast fashion. I just can't believe how short-sighted they are.

I've made up my mind. I'm going through before we shut it down. I know I can never return. I'll wave at the drone camera
on the other side to let the team know I made it. After they see my confirmation - or my expiration - they've been
instructed to close the portal and comply fully with the authorities.

This is my life's work. I will, literally, go wherever the science takes me. Wish me luck.
